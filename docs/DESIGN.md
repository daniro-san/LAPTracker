## Who uses this, and in what language

Members of a game alliance on server 325 — Russia, Spain, Brazil, the United States and more — on phones, in a Discord thread, often during or right after the event. Most are not native English readers; some read no English at all. Translation is a later phase (after Phase 8); **being usable without it is a requirement now.** The test is literal: a member who speaks no English opens the page and, within ten seconds, finds their alliance, sees whether might and kills went up or down and by how much, and knows when the numbers are from.

### Language-light rules

- **Numbers first, words second.** Every data row reads as `label · number · signed delta with arrow · percent`. The label is one word, the same word the game's own UI uses (Might, Kills, Members), because the players already know those words in the game. In a language whose game word is sourced (ko, vi, ru: `lib/i18n/glossary/<tag>.md`) the label is that word; elsewhere it stays the game's English word (D52).
- **Three channels for every state**, never one: colour **and** an arrow/sign **and** a short word. Green alone, an arrow alone or English alone is not enough. Colour-blind safe by construction.
- **One legend, once.** A compact legend (arrow up/down, same, rank, the stale mark, the rounded mark, and the `*` of a change that includes members who left, only where a change carries it) sits under the header; nothing else explains itself in a sentence.
- **A mark only when something is wrong** (Session 7d, 07c S2). Rows carry `○ Stale` or `✗ Failed` when their data is; good data carries no mark, so history rows do not repeat a word that means nothing weeks later.
- **The game's four words stay in blank mode** (D23, 07c N1, D52): Might, Kills, Members and Level are the labels members read in the game's own UI, so the ten-second test keeps them, in the page's language where the game's word is sourced; every other string is blanked.
- **Sentences only in the margins.** The grandma voice lives in empty states, error pages, section titles and the admin area. Status is chips, not prose: `Last updated at Oct 31, 14:01 server` and `Next update at …` (owner, 2026-11-05: these replace the earlier `Before ✓ 12:01` · `After ✓ 16:01` chips). A public page has no paragraph.
- **`/brand` is exempt from the paragraph rule** (07c N7, #54): it is the brand guidelines for whoever builds the site, `noindex` and linked from nowhere, so its rules and "when to use" lines are sentences. Its strings still live in `lib/copy-brand.ts`, and its React keys never come from copy.
- **Dates and numbers through `Intl`** with the request's `Accept-Language`: `16:01` not `4:01 PM`, `3,94B` for pt-BR, `3.94B` for en (compact numbers use K, M, B, T in every language and keep the locale's decimal separator, D78). Every public time is server time and says so (`16:01 server time`) unless the viewer chose their own zone in the settings gear; then it reads `16:01 your time`, and a table corner reads `Your time` (D38). No UTC twin (owner, 2026-11-05). Dates follow the locale unless the viewer chose an order (`31 Oct`, `Oct 31`, `31/10`, `31.10`, `2026-10-31`); the month name stays in the locale's language. /admin and Discord always use server time. Relative times (`4 days ago`) are secondary.
- **Universal symbols where they are truly universal**: ▲ ▼ for change, an outlined dot for stale, a magnifier for search, ✓ ✗ for done/failed. No icons that need a culture to read (no thumbs, no wink, no skull for kills — "Kills" is already the game's word). One exception (owner, 2026-11-06, D54): the Movers tabs, four to a 360 px row, lead with a drawn mark — boxing glove for Might, skull for Kills, trophy for Rank — and show the word beside it from 640 px; on a phone the open tab's word heads its panel, and every tab's word is read out.
- **Every string in `lib/copy.ts`**, none in components, so translation later is a file, not a refactor. Keys are stable identifiers; the value is the English text.
- **The ledger is for the whole server, not for OGs.** Other alliances in the top 10 will use it, so nothing is pinned: order is strictly by rank, and rank numbers (`#1 … #20`) are shown because they are language-free. A viewer may pick _their_ alliance once (tap the tag → "This is my alliance"); it is highlighted and gets a "jump to" control, remembered in `localStorage` for that browser only — a per-viewer convenience, never server state, and the page is correct without it.

## Overview

A guild ledger, kept by hand, in a plague year. The page is aged paper; the ink is near-black; the header is dark plum; a wax-seal red marks losses and warnings; yarn marks gains, bronze in daylight and gold by lamplight. Craft shows in the margins — a crochet lace border on the header, a ball of yarn in one corner, a teacup where there is nothing to show — and nowhere else. **The data table stays plain**: high contrast, tabular numerals, one-pixel rules, no texture behind a number. If an ornament ever competes with a number, the ornament goes.

**Key characteristics**

- Paper, not screen: flat `{colors.paper}` canvas, `{colors.rule}` hairlines, no gradients, no glow.
- Alegreya for page titles, the wordmark and error lines; Alegreya Sans 700 for section and card titles; Source Sans 3 for everything else, numerals always `tabular-nums`.
- Colour means something: sign of a delta, a warning, a neutral badge. Decoration never uses the accent colours.
- Square corners (`{rounded.none}`) on tables and cards, 2 px on controls; the one circle is the wax seal.
- Mobile first: designed at 360 px, cards below 640 px, every target ≥ 44 px, nothing hover-only.
- Dark mode is the same ledger by lamplight — swapped token values, nothing redesigned. It follows the system until the viewer picks a theme in the header's settings gear; that choice is kept in a `theme` cookie (per browser, never server state) so the server renders the right mode on first paint.

**The muted-text floor (issue 585).** Muted text is never lighter than ink at 70 % (`text-ink/70`: 5.72:1 on paper, 7.22:1 in dark), so the two themes pass alike; ink/55 and ink/60 (3.62:1 and 4.17:1 in light) are not used for text. A placeholder is the field's own text at 70 % (`::placeholder` in `app/globals.css`) and only a hint: every field has a visible label. Something past or set aside is never faded with `opacity`, which drops a row and its live buttons below 3:1: it says so in a word or a frame, at full contrast: an event whose time has come reads "· Started" in plum, then "· Ended" in ink/75 with its title at regular weight and ink/75 (`Ended.tsx`, written like "· Today"); a left-out row has a dashed frame; a day of another month in the month grid is ink/70 in a dashed frame. A disabled control is the one thing still dimmed. Values on the desk, the board and a member's page read at 14 px or more (`body-sm`); 12 px stays for labels (`{typography.label}`, tags, weekday heads, chart axes). An empty hero star is an outline at ink/70, not a faint fill.

## Typography

- **Alegreya 400** (OFL, Google Fonts via `next/font/google`, `display: swap`, subsets latin, latin-ext, cyrillic, cyrillic-ext, vietnamese, greek) — headings, the wordmark, empty-state and error-page lines. One weight with a calligraphic pen stroke that sits with Alegreya Sans and the drawings; synthetic bold is off (`font-synthesis-weight: none`). Never below 20 px. Decided on 2026-11-10 (brand review H2): one display face that covers Latin, Vietnamese, Cyrillic and Greek, so no title falls back to a system serif per language; it replaced Young Serif (latin only), which was chosen at the Session 7 review. Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai titles use the named serifs after it in `--font-display` (Hiragino Mincho ProN, Songti SC, Noto Serif CJK SC, Noto Serif Thai, Georgia).
- **Alegreya Sans 700** (OFL, `next/font/google`) — secondary display: section titles (`{typography.display-md}`), card titles and the empty-state line (`{typography.display-sm}`). A calligraphic humanist sans whose pen-like strokes sit with the drawings.
- **Source Sans 3** (OFL) — body, tables, controls. Numerals are `{typography.numeral}`: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, right-aligned, compact (`3.94B`, `+20.1M`) with the full value in a `title`/tap.
- **The Discord event picture** (D58) draws the same three families with next/og, from static TTFs committed in `lib/brand/fonts/` with their OFL licences: Satori reads TTF, OTF and WOFF but not the WOFF2 that `next/font` serves. A name's characters outside Latin, Greek and Cyrillic, and emoji, are left out of the picture so the renderer never downloads a font.
- Three families, each with one job (owner, Session 7 review; display face changed 2026-11-10): Alegreya (primary display), Alegreya Sans (secondary display), Source Sans 3 (text and numbers). No fourth. No italics for emphasis in headings, no all-caps display; `{typography.label}` is the only tracked, uppercase style.

## Layout

- **Spacing**: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 (`{spacing.xxs}` … `{spacing.section}`). Page gutter `{spacing.md}` on phones, `{spacing.lg}` above 640 px.
- **Breakpoints**: `< 640px` the comparison table becomes one `stat-card` per alliance; `≥ 640px` the full table. One component, two layouts. Horizontal scroll only if twenty columns truly do not fit at 1280 — try first. The page layout changes at 768, 1024 and 1440 (below).

### Layout system (D122)

Mobile first does not mean phone only (owner, 2026-11-17, issue 412). A page is designed at 360 px and then laid out again at 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440, so tablets and laptops use their width and pages get shorter. One system for the desk, the members' board and the public pages, built from the layout components below, never from per-page grid classes.

**Widths.** Tailwind `sm` 640 stays the cards-to-table switch; `md` 768, `lg` 1024 and `xl` 1280 (Tailwind's default, kept) are the layout steps; one added breakpoint, `wide` 1440 (`--breakpoint-wide: 90rem` in `tokens.css`), is used only for the extra table columns and the 32 px margin. Amended 2026-11-19 (coordinator, under the owner's carte blanche): the sidebar starts at 1280, not 1440, because most laptops are 1280–1439 wide.

| Width       | Desk navigation                         | Margin | Gutter | Content                                                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 360 – 767   | bottom tabs (today's five)              | 16     | 16     | one column; cards; `stat-card`s below 640                              |
| 768 – 1023  | 88 px rail, icon over a label (2 lines) | 24     | 16     | one column, but paired blocks sit two-up (`Split even`, `CardGrid`)    |

- **Page width.** Content is at most 1200 px (`--page-max`), centred in what the navigation leaves. Prose (notices, Start here, help lines, a member's notes) stays within 65 characters (`--measure`, Baymard: 50–75), and a form within 560 px (`--form-max`), wherever the column is wider. Nothing is stretched to the edge of a 1440 screen.
- **Header.** The plum (public) and burgundy (desk) bars stay full width. The public bar is `--masthead`, deep plum in both themes (#3b2436, in dark #55344e) with wax-paper text (11.35:1 and 8.5:1), so it stays deep plum by lamplight instead of taking the pale dark-mode plum (issue 473); its lace hangs in the same colour. Their content starts where the page content starts (after the rail or sidebar on the desk) and ends at the content's right edge.
- **The ledger spread.** At ≥ 1024 a page is a main column (2/3) and a margin column (1/3) on its right, divided by a 2 px ink rule, like the written page and the margin of a ledger. The main column holds the one dominant thing of the page (the ledger, the member list, the week, the notice being written) in drawn cards. The margin column holds what is read beside it — the figures, leadership, biggest changes, legend, counts, recent activity — as **margin notes**: no frame, a ruled head (`{typography.label}` over a 2 px ink rule) and hairline rows. The margin column is sticky when it is shorter than the main column; a sticky margin is at most the window's height less its 16 px from the top and the page's bottom padding, and scrolls on its own with the thin scrollbar, so a note that grows never makes the page longer (issue 412 UX pass). The HTML order is always the phone order; grid areas move the margin column to the right, so a phone, a screen reader and the keyboard read the same order everywhere. The margin column is never on the left.
- **One dominant thing per page.** No row of equal-weight KPI tiles, no three equal cards across, no hero. If two blocks compete, one goes into the margin column.
- **Master-detail** (Members, Follow-up, Former members). At ≥ 1024 the list (7/12) and the open item (5/12, sticky) sit side by side; the open row is tinted plum 15 % with a 3 px plum bar (11av persona check: 10 % read taupe in light mode; as the navigation's tint); the open item is in the URL (`?m=<uid>`), so back and a shared link work. Below 1024 the same item opens in the `Sheet`, as today. The detail pane is the sheet's content: one component, two containers. With nothing open the pane shows one muted line ("Pick a member to see their info."), never an empty frame. Built for Members and Former members in 11av (rollout PR 3): from 1024 a press on a row, or Enter on it, opens the member in the pane and pushes `?m=` (back goes to the member before, then to none); Up and Down move the focus to the next row and open it there, replacing the address rather than adding a step; the focus stays in the list, and the pane's Close gives it back to the open row. Below 1024 the open member is also in the address (replaced, not pushed: the sheet owns its history entry as before), so a shared link opens the sheet on a phone. A resize across 1024 moves the open member between the sheet and the pane. The pane sticks 8 px from the top, level with the list's search field, and is at most the window's height less 40 px; its bar's two buttons never break a label and take a row each when they do not fit side by side (PT at 1024); so at the end of the list the sticky pane never loses its title; nothing follows the list and its pane on the page (Members puts "Import the old spreadsheets" at the end of the list's column).
- **Tables grow by priority.** A `ResponsiveTable` declares each column's priority: always, from 640, from 1024, from 1440 (`wide`). Cards below 640; the head row is sticky wherever the table shows, under the list's own sticky search bar (11av: from 640, not only from 1024, because Members' column labels used to stick in its search bar at every width); at ≥ 1440 the low-priority columns appear (on Members, from the data the list already has: kills and main squad beside might, or the two the order does not show; roll calls and last talk are not in the list's data). Tables shown one under another (Members' groups) set their number columns' widths so they line up. The name column never wraps a tag under itself; it truncates nothing either: the name wraps, tags and numbers do not.
- **Page head.** Title and meta line on the left; the page's actions on the right from 768, on one row. Below 768 the one primary action shows and the rest go under "More ▾" (a `<details>` menu of 44 px rows), so the first content row of a phone screen is the content, not six rows of buttons.
- **Nothing short wraps** (persona panel, round 2). A change keeps its arrow, value and percent on one line (`nowrap`; the percent goes under the whole change, never under half of it); a time, a count tag and a row's tag do not wrap; a long name ends in an ellipsis before its tag drops to a second line, and its full text is the link's accessible name. A row of sort or filter chips is one row that scrolls sideways inside itself when it is wider than its column (the existing `member-filter` rule, now at every width), so no chip sits alone on a second line. Page actions that do not fit beside the title take their own row under it (1024–1279 on the desk). A card's link ("Open Calendar") goes to the card's foot below 768, so the title keeps the line.
- **Even rhythm in the margin column**: 32 px above every margin note's head; a group of people in the margin (Leadership) is a list, and two-up only where each cell is at least 200 px wide, with the name on the first line and the role and figures under it.
- **Touch stays.** Rows are 44 px or taller at every width: tablets and touch laptops use the wide layouts too. No density switch, no hover-only control, no tooltip.
- **Desk navigation.** The five tabs stay the five destinations. On the rail and the sidebar the tab keeps its icon and word; the sidebar also lists each tab's views (Members: Former members; Calendar: Caravans, Notices, Thefts) and, under a hairline at the end, "See what members see" (the members' board). Below 1280 the views are reached by the in-page `view-switch` as today; from 1280 the in-page copies (Calendar's Week · Caravans · Notices · Thefts with its "See what members see", Members · Former members) are hidden, so a view is offered once (11aq persona check). A page names its open view to the frame through `current` (`"notices"` opens Calendar with Notices marked). The public pages keep their header: they have no destinations to rail.
- **Charts at wide widths (D68).** A chart takes the width of its column, never the page: in the main column at most 8 of 12 columns, in the margin column its full width. Height steps with width: `h-32` up to 1023, 160 px from 1024; `small` stays `h-16`. Labels stay HTML, numbers stay beside the chart; where the margin column exists, the chart's numbers (summary line or readings table) sit beside it rather than under Details. HTML bars (`server-pulse`) keep at most 24 px per bar and a 2 px gap; with more width they do not get fatter, the busiest span gets more room for its words. A tap target is still 44 px, so a wider chart does not add tap points.
- **Measured before and after** (11ap audit, seed data, light, EN): the public alliance page is 7,497 px tall at 360, 4,052 at 768 and 3,857 at 1024 and 1440 because it never uses more than 960 px; the mock of the spread is 1,819 at 1024 and 1,746 at 1440. Desk Members is 2,378 px at every width from 768 up, its list one 960 px column with two numbers.
- **Touch**: every interactive element ≥ 44 px; focus ring `{colors.plum}` 2 px with 2 px offset; on a Details bar (the `details-bar` class) the ring goes round the framed "Details ▾" control only, never the whole bar (issue 125); everything reachable by keyboard.

Below 1024, where there is no margin column, the same block wears the `drawn-card` frame with the desk cards' padding (16 × 12 px), so a phone still sees cards.
- **`CardGrid`** — equal cards in columns: `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(100%, var(--card-min)), 1fr))` with the gutter; one column at 360, two at 768, three only where cards are short (notices, roll call kinds). Reading order is row by row, so it is for items without an order; ordered lists (the members, a week's days) stay one list.
- **`MasterDetail`** — `list` and `detail` slots, the open item from the URL. Below 1024 it renders only the list and the caller's `Sheet` opens the detail; from 1024 the detail pane is sticky beside the list (7/5) with the open row marked by the caller (`aria-current="true"` on the row). The pane is the same `Sheet` with `pane` (title row, content and sticky bar in a framed box that scrolls on its own, with the thin scrollbar). The address, the width and the arrow keys are in `app/(private)/_ui/open-member.ts` (`useOpenMember`, `useWide`, `stepRow`).
- **`ResponsiveTable`** — the `stat-table` rule made general: columns with `priority` (`always`, `sm`, `lg`, `wide`) and an optional `width`, a card renderer for below 640 (`compact`: rows divided by hairlines, not spaced cards), a sticky head row (its top from `--table-head-top`, under a sticky bar), numerals right-aligned and tabular, rows ≥ 44 px. With `onRow` a press anywhere on a row opens it; the row's button in its first cell is the keyboard's way in, its click reaching the row. `StatTable`, the members list, the run trace and the history tables move onto it.
- **`Measure`** — wraps prose to `--measure` (65ch) and forms to `--form-max`; used by notices, Start here, how-to, the PIN gate and every sheet's body when it shows in a pane.

## Workspace components

The officers' workspace (`/w/[slug]`, D71) uses the components above plus these. Build new screens from them; a new kind of control needs a line here first.

- **Sheet frame** — every workspace sheet is `Sheet` (`lib/brand/components/Sheet.tsx`) with `title` and `words` (`sheetWords(t)`, `app/(private)/_ui/sheet-words.ts`):
  - a sticky title row: the title (display-sm) and a 44 px Close (×) at the right. The title takes the first focus only from code and draws no ring (`[tabindex="-1"]:focus`, `app/globals.css`); a child marked `data-initial-focus` goes first;
  - with `primary`, a sticky bar at the bottom: the main action first, `button-primary`, widest (`flex-1`); then Cancel, `button-secondary`. `secondary` replaces Cancel where the sheet has another second action ("Clear filters"). `note` sits above the buttons: why the main action is off, the last error. A sheet without a main action (a picker, settings) has no bar: Close is at the top. A sheet that shows a member's facts (a member, a new member, a former member) puts "Edit info" (main) and "Paste what they told you" in its bar (`factsActions`, `MemberCards.tsx`), so they stay in view while the content scrolls; other actions on that sheet ("Link to a member", Remove) stay in the content, since the bar holds two buttons;
  - the sheet scrolls as one; the title row and the bar stay put. Its scrollbar, the page's and a scrolling margin pane's, is thin (`scrollbar-width: thin`, `::-webkit-scrollbar` 8 px for Safari): a thumb of `--ink` at 35 % on no track, so it reads in light and dark. Never hidden: on a desktop it is how a sheet shows there is more (`app/globals.css`);
  - `dirty` (the form differs from what it opened with, `changedFrom` in `sheet-close.ts`): Close, Cancel, Escape, a tap on the backdrop and the phone's back gesture all ask "Discard your changes?" in the bar first, with "Keep editing" as the main button;

## Motion

Only where it carries meaning or a smile: the error-page illustrations (one keyframe loop each, 2–4 s), the search's pending magnifier (one 3 s loop, shown only after 300 ms of waiting), the freshness dot while a run is live, the admin live candle while a run is in progress (flame sway 0.9 s, glow flicker 1.3 s; `live-indicator`), the bottom sheet entering (200 ms ease-out). Everything stops under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No page-load animation, no parallax, no shimmer skeletons; a wait elsewhere is a plain "Loading the ledger…" line.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Lead with paper and ink; let plum, seal and yarn each mean one thing.
- Keep every number in `{typography.numeral}`, right-aligned, compact, full value on tap.
- Put craft in the margins: header, corner, seals, empty states, error pages.
- Design at 360 px first, then lay the page out again at 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440 (D122); screenshot 360 / 768 / 1024 / 1280 / 1440 in both modes.
- Change this file in the same commit as the tokens when a decision changes.
- Run the ten-second test with a real member who reads no English before calling a page done.

### Don't

- Inter, Roboto or system-ui for display; a fourth typeface for anything.
- Gradients, glassmorphism, blur, glow, `shadow-lg`, `rounded-2xl`, pill buttons.
- Hero sections, three-column feature grids, numbered "01 / 02 / 03" labels, italic accent words.
- Emoji or sparkles in UI copy; generic illustration packs; charts (v1 has none).
- Paragraphs on public pages; status as prose; a label longer than two words; information that exists only in a tooltip or only in a colour.
- A dark mode that is a different design; a component that exists only once and only here.
- A page's own grid, max-width or breakpoint classes for its frame; a margin column on the left; a row of equal KPI tiles; content stretched to the edge of a wide screen (D122).

## Validation (owner, 2026-11-16 to 2026-11-21)

- **People first (D75).** Every workspace feature makes the day easier for leadership and members; nothing punishes people. Thanks come first. Missing data is "no data", never 0, never flagged and never in a comparison. No ranking of people and no automatic consequences. Real signals reach the leadership as check-in reasons with their evidence, and the leadership decides. Check every screen, string and persona test against this.
- **Design freedom.** Designer and frontend personas may rethink a layout, or add a reusable component to this system, instead of patching a page.
- **Three checks before a design change is built.** A change to how something is presented or used is built only when all three agree, and the PR records the evidence:
  - the user personas, officer panel first;
  - real usage in PostHog, read only: pageviews, events and drop-offs for the screens touched;
  - a product-strategy persona.
- **Measure before building.** The product-strategy persona reviews the issue's "What will we want to measure after release?" field before building, or proposes the events when it is empty (CONTRIBUTING.md § Working rules, "Analytics plan, every change").
- **Every persona panel includes a product-strategy persona.** It proposes changes and new uses of the data, not only critique.
- **The officer panel.** Experienced players of this kind of game, aged 20s to 50s, from several countries, some native English readers and some not. They are not novices, but they lack our context about the platform. Don't over-simplify, and don't leave things implicit. Every flow is clear; where it can't be, add in-place help (a hint, a short guide, a GIF or a video). Check reading level, contrast and text size for older eyes.
- **Panels see real public data, locally, and never private fields.** A persona panel is an AI request, so the CLAUDE.md roster-fields rule applies. Panels may see real public data (names, might, kills, what the public pages show) on the local machine. They never get `x`, `y`, `online`, `last_online_at`, `joined_at`, `daily_*`, `active` or `avatar_url`, not even locally (`.claude/rules/private.md`). Nothing a panel sees is committed, published, logged or posted, and the raw files are deleted afterwards.
- **UX review before the owner.** Every chart or visualization PR gets a designer review of the real app and the Discord render, and its fixes land before the owner reads the PR. It covers:
  - phones at 360, 390 and 412 in WebKit and Chrome;
  - tablets in portrait at 600 to 820, and in landscape at 1024 and 1180 (iPad in WebKit);
  - 1280;
  - light and dark;
  - a long-word language.

  It checks overflow, 44 px targets, contrast, truncation, and the one sentence a reader should take away in 3 seconds. A problem at a phone width blocks the PR; one that shows only at 640 px or wider is secondary. Lead with the answer and keep controls compact.

- **First-glance test (owner, 2026-11-21, issue 585).** Every screen, route, component, modal and feature is checked this way before it ships. Each persona gets a real question the screen exists to answer ("Do we have shields recorded for today's raid?", "Who do I still need to remind?", "Is this the right raid?") and sees only the first viewport at 360 × 780 for about 5 seconds: a screenshot of the first screen, nothing below it. The persona says what they conclude. A wrong conclusion is a MUST, even when a careful reading of the screen would get it right. Run it before and after the change, and record each question with each persona's answer in the PR. It came from an R5 who read Raid prep's date line, the day the roll call was added, as an old roll call, and concluded that no shields were recorded for the raid.
